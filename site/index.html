Federal contracts awarded under "urgency"

Contracts the government awarded in fiscal year 2026 without full and open competition by citing "unusual and compelling urgency" (FAR 6.302-2). Orders that were actually competed off an existing contract vehicle are excluded — they only inherit the urgency code from their parent (see "What is this & how it's counted" below). Click any row for detail; every award links to its USAspending page.

What is this & how it's counted

Agencies normally must compete contracts. One legal way to skip that is to claim "unusual and compelling urgency" — the standard being that the government would suffer serious injury, financial or otherwise, without it. On each award's USAspending page, the designation appears under Additional Details → Competition Details as "Other than Full and Open Competition: Urgency." That's the reason code, not the written justification.

These are all FAR 6.302-2 awards, and they come in a few flavors — shown in the Type column: definitive contracts (a new standalone contract), delivery orders (an order placed against an existing contract vehicle), and purchase orders (small simplified buys). A written Justification & Approval (J&A) gets posted to SAM.gov mainly for the standalone contracts; for a delivery order the justification usually lives at the parent vehicle, and purchase orders fall below the threshold — so most awards here have no posted J&A, and the 📄 Justification badge marks the ones that do (an amber 📄 Parent J&A means the justification we found is the parent vehicle's, not the order's).

What's excluded, and why the total is lower than the raw FPDS figure: we drop orders that were actually competed among a contract vehicle's awardees — FPDS marks these "fair opportunity given." They carry the "urgency" code only because it's inherited from their parent contract, so they were not awarded without competition and don't belong in a no-competition tracker. That removes roughly $21B of border-wall delivery orders that were competed at the order level — which is why this shows ~$6B, not the ~$27B you'd get by filtering the urgency code alone. Also not included: orders that cite urgency under FAR 16.505 (a separate "fair opportunity exception" field).
